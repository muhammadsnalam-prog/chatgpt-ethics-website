Homepage / Introduction

What technology did we choose, and why does it matter?

Our team chose ChatGPT, a generative artificial intelligence chatbot developed by OpenAI. ChatGPT can answer questions, explain concepts, summarize information, brainstorm ideas, draft writing, generate code, and support communication tasks through natural language.

We chose ChatGPT because it is no longer just a futuristic idea. It is already being used by students, teachers, businesses, and everyday users. This makes ChatGPT important not only as a technical system, but also as a social force that affects learning, work, trust, privacy, creativity, and accountability.

💡

Why it matters

ChatGPT can make information and writing support more accessible, but it can also create new risks when people overtrust it.

🌎

Who it affects

Students, educators, workers, customers, businesses, developers, and policymakers all have a stake in how ChatGPT is used.

⚖️

Main claim

ChatGPT should support human judgment, not replace human responsibility.

Technology Explanation

How does ChatGPT work?

ChatGPT is powered by a large language model. In simple terms, it learns patterns from large amounts of text and uses those patterns to predict useful responses to a user’s prompt.

1. User enters a prompt
2. Model analyzes context
3. Model predicts response
4. User receives output
5. Human verifies result
Important limitation: ChatGPT can sound confident even when it is wrong. This is why human review is essential in school, business, healthcare, law, and other serious settings.
Applications of the Technology

Real-world applications of ChatGPT

We focus on two real-world contexts where ChatGPT-style tools are already being used: education and customer service/business productivity.

Application 1: Education — ChatGPT as a learning assistant

Example: ChatGPT Edu is designed for universities to deploy AI to students, faculty, researchers, and campus operations.

In education, ChatGPT can help students understand hard topics, summarize readings, brainstorm ideas, practice questions, and receive tutoring-style explanations outside normal class time. Teachers and faculty can also use AI for feedback, planning, and administrative support.

Benefits
  • Faster study support and personalized explanations
  • Help for students who are stuck outside class hours
  • Support for faculty productivity and campus operations
Ethical complexity
  • Students may submit AI-generated work as their own
  • Overdependence can weaken writing, memory, and critical thinking
  • Unequal access to paid tools can create unfair advantages

Application 2: Customer Service — AI assistants in business

Example: Klarna’s OpenAI-powered assistant handled millions of customer conversations in its first month.

In customer service, ChatGPT-style tools can answer questions, summarize issues, help with refunds or returns, draft responses, and support workers. Businesses may use these tools to speed up service and reduce repetitive work.

Benefits
  • Faster answers for customers
  • Lower workload for support teams
  • 24/7 support across languages and markets
Ethical complexity
  • AI may give incorrect or misleading answers
  • Customers may not know when they are talking to AI
  • Automation can create job disruption and accountability concerns
Social Challenges

What makes ChatGPT complicated and debatable?

ChatGPT is complicated because its benefits and harms often appear at the same time. The same tool that helps a student understand a concept can also help another student cheat. The same tool that speeds up customer service can also mislead customers or reduce human job opportunities.

Why responsible use is difficult

  • Accuracy: ChatGPT can hallucinate or invent details.
  • Transparency: Users may not know how an answer was produced.
  • Privacy: People may paste sensitive school, customer, or company data into AI tools.
  • Accountability: When AI causes a mistake, responsibility can become unclear.
  • Human skills: If people rely on AI too much, they may practice less independent thinking.
Critical Discussion / Ethical Lenses

Two ethical lenses: utilitarianism and deontology

We use two ethical lenses because they reveal different sides of the same technology. Utilitarianism looks at outcomes. Deontology looks at duties, rules, and responsibilities.

⚖️

Lens 1: Utilitarianism

Core question: Does ChatGPT create more overall benefit than harm?

From this view, ChatGPT can be ethical if it improves learning, saves time, supports workers, reduces repetitive tasks, and makes useful information easier to access.

But if it causes cheating, misinformation, privacy violations, or harmful job disruption, the harms may outweigh the benefits.

📜

Lens 2: Deontology

Core question: Are people using ChatGPT according to duties, rules, and rights?

From this view, students have a duty to be honest, businesses have a duty to avoid misleading customers, and developers have a duty to reduce risks and improve transparency.

Even if ChatGPT creates benefits, it can still be unethical if it is used dishonestly or without respect for privacy, consent, and accountability.

Application Context Utilitarian Analysis Deontological Analysis
Education Helpful if it improves learning and access to support more than it harms academic integrity. Students and teachers must follow clear rules about disclosure, authorship, and honest work.
Customer Service Helpful if it improves service speed and quality more than it creates misinformation or job harm. Companies should be transparent when AI is used and must protect customer rights and data.
Privacy Productivity benefits do not justify careless exposure of sensitive personal or business information. Organizations have a duty to protect data, get consent where needed, and limit unnecessary collection.
Social Implications

What social, political, and cultural concerns does ChatGPT create?

🗳️

Political influence

ChatGPT can help people understand public issues, but it can also generate persuasive misinformation, biased arguments, or manipulative political content.

💼

Economic impact

AI can improve productivity, but it also changes job expectations and may reduce demand for some repetitive communication or support roles.

🎨

Cultural change

ChatGPT changes how people learn, write, create, and communicate. Society must decide when AI help is useful and when it weakens human creativity.

Recommendations

How can ChatGPT be used in a less harmful way?

Our recommendation is not to ban ChatGPT. Instead, organizations should use clear rules, human oversight, transparency, privacy protection, and risk management.

For schools and teachers

  • Create clear AI-use policies for assignments.
  • Require drafts, reflections, oral explanations, or process-based work.
  • Teach students how to verify AI responses and cite AI use when required.

For students

  • Use ChatGPT as a tutor, not as a replacement for learning.
  • Do not submit AI-generated work as if it is fully your own.
  • Use it for brainstorming, review, practice questions, and explanations.

For businesses

  • Review AI-generated customer responses before relying on them.
  • Tell customers when they are interacting with AI.
  • Protect sensitive customer and company data.

For developers and policymakers

  • Test AI systems for bias, privacy risks, and unsafe outputs.
  • Design AI tools with transparency and auditability.
  • Create rules for high-impact settings like education, finance, healthcare, and public services.
Strong responsible-use standard: ChatGPT should be used as a supervised assistant, not as an unchecked decision-maker.

Conclusion

Final takeaway

ChatGPT is a powerful example of advanced information technology. It can support education, improve communication, speed up customer service, and help people work faster. At the same time, it creates serious concerns about misinformation, bias, privacy, academic honesty, job impact, and accountability.

Final argument: ChatGPT is most ethical when humans stay informed, responsible, and in control.
